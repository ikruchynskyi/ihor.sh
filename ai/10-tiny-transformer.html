AI from scratch · Chapter 10

A tiny transformer, trained here

Chapter 7 drew attention as pictures. This chapter builds the real thing: a GPT, the same design as the models behind chatbots, shrunk to one block and about 36,000 numbers so it trains in your browser in a minute. It reads every subway station name in New York, one character at a time, learning to guess the next one. Then it makes up stations that don't exist, but sound like they should.

1. The task: guess the next letter

Language models do one thing: given the text so far, give a probability for every possible next piece. Chatbots predict word-pieces; ours predicts single characters, from an alphabet of 68 (letters, digits, a few symbols, and a "the name ends here" mark). Here's everything it will learn from:

Before training, the model knows nothing, so it gives all 68 characters the same chance. Its loss is −ln(the chance it gave the right one). What's that loss?

\(-\ln(1/68) = \ln 68 \approx 4.22\). That's the line to beat: every step of learning shows up as the loss falling below it. (Chapter 5 is where this loss comes from.)

2. The model

"Court Sq" → character + position embeddings → attention: each letter looks back at the earlier ones + residual → MLP: 48 → 192 → 48, ReLU + residual → scores for 68 next characters

Each character becomes a vector of 48 numbers (its embedding, plus one for its position). Attention lets every position gather information from the positions before it, weighted by how relevant they are; the MLP then thinks about what it gathered. The residual connections ("+") add each step's result to its input, so information can flow straight through. Big models stack this block 50–100 times, with thousands of numbers per vector and dozens of attention heads; ours has one of each.

\[ \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}} + \text{mask}\right) V, \qquad Q = XW_q,\; K = XW_k,\; V = XW_v \]

The mask sets every "look at a later position" score to −∞ before the softmax, so a letter can't peek at the answer it's supposed to predict.

Why does training need the mask, but a model that only ever generates left to right could seem not to?

  • It makes training faster
  • In training, all positions are predicted at once from a known text; without the mask, each could just read the next letter from its neighbor
  • Softmax needs it
Training scores every position of a window in one pass. If position 5 could attend to position 6, predicting position 6 would be copying, and the model would learn nothing useful. The mask makes each position predict from the past only, exactly as it must when generating.

3. Train it

Each step takes 8 random 32-character windows of the station list, predicts every next character, measures the loss, and nudges all 36,000 numbers downhill (backpropagation through the attention and the MLP, then the Adam optimizer: chapters 2 and 4). Every 50 steps it makes up a few new names.

Train for a few hundred steps, then make some up.

Green: a name that isn't a real station. Gray: one it memorized from the list.

Turn the temperature down to 0.2 and generate. Why does it start repeating real stations?

Temperature divides the scores before the softmax. Low temperature sharpens the probabilities toward the single most likely next letter, so the model follows the strongest paths it learned, which are the real names. High temperature flattens them, so it takes unlikely turns: more original, and more nonsense ("Qxv St"). Creativity in a language model is literally this knob.

That's the whole idea, at toy scale. The GPTs behind chatbots are this block, repeated dozens of times, about a million times wider, trained on trillions of word-pieces instead of 379 station names. Same arithmetic.
