AI from scratch

How machine learning actually works, built up from pictures you can drag: first the math (vectors, matrices, derivatives, gradients, integrals, probability), then neural networks you can watch learn, then the ideas behind today's models: convolutions, attention, agents and vision. Every idea comes three ways: a picture you can drag, the math behind it worked out with real numbers, and code you can edit and run right in the page to build it yourself, from a dot product up to an autograd engine and a transformer block. Questions along the way ask you to predict before you're told.

The road goes from zero to ML engineer: Part 1 is how models learn; Parts 2–5 are what the job adds: data and evaluation, training in practice, building with LLMs, and running models in production. Faded chapters are planned and on their way.

Foundations · Calculus for ML

Chapters 2 and 4 give the intuition; these four lessons give the tools: the rules, many variables, curvature and integrals, each with sliders, questions and code.

  1. Derivatives: the rules of change

    Secants turning into tangents, the seven rules, eˣ and log-probabilities, activation slopes and vanishing gradients, and gradient checking.

  2. Many inputs: partials, gradients, Jacobians

    Slopes on a landscape you drag over, the gradients of MSE and weight matrices, the Jacobian as a local matrix, and backprop as vector-Jacobian products.

  3. Taylor series and curvature

    Polynomials that hug a curve, why the learning rate must stay under 2/k, zigzags in stretched valleys, Newton's method, and saddles.

  4. Integrals: adding up the infinitely small

    Strips under a curve, the fundamental theorem, densities and the bell curve, and why a minibatch's noise shrinks like 1/√N.

Part 1 · How models learn

  1. Matrices are moves

    Vectors as arrows, adding tip to tail, a matrix as a move of the whole plane, the determinant as area, and a network layer as "move, then fold".

  2. Gradients: which way is downhill

    Derivatives as slopes you can feel, the gradient as an arrow, gradient descent rolling a ball into a valley.

  3. A neuron from scratch

    Weights, a bias and a bend: one neuron learning to separate two clouds of points, live.

  4. Backprop you can watch

    The chain rule as a relay of blame through a small network, step by step.

  5. Probability and loss

    Softmax, cross-entropy, and why a model "guesses" instead of knowing.

  6. Seeing: convolutions

    Filters sliding over images, feature maps, and a digit recognizer trained in your browser.

  7. Attention and transformers

    Words as vectors, attention as weighted averages, a tiny transformer you can poke.

  8. Agents

    Tools, planning and evals, using Blip, the companion on this site, as the worked example.

  9. Vision in practice

    Detection and segmentation on real camera images, including NYC traffic cameras.

  10. A tiny transformer

    A real GPT, one block and 36,000 numbers, trained in your browser on NYC subway station names.
