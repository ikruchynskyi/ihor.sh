AI from scratch

One path from zero to junior ML engineer. If you can do school arithmetic, you can start: stage 0 gives you just enough code and math, and every idea after that comes three ways: a picture you drag, the math worked out with real numbers, and Python code you edit and run right in the page, plus a practice notebook for every lesson. Questions along the way ask you to predict before you're told.

Review deck · Glossary (every term on the path)

Take the lessons in order: each one only leans on the ones before it, and every lesson ends with a link to the next. Each stage ends with a milestone project. Faded lessons are planned and on their way.

Stage 1 · The math of learning

Vectors and matrices, derivatives and gradients, probability and data, all as pictures first.

  1. Chapter 1Matrices are moves

    Vectors as arrows, a matrix as a move of the whole plane, the determinant as area, a network layer as "move, then fold".

  2. Linear algebra 1Systems of equations, rank and inverses

    Equations as lines and planes you drag, elimination step by step, rank, and which matrices can be undone, and linear regression solved exactly.

  3. Chapter 2Gradients: which way is downhill

    Derivatives as slopes you can feel, the gradient as an arrow, a ball rolling into a valley.

  4. Calculus 1Derivatives: the rules of change

    Secants turning into tangents, the seven rules, eˣ and log-probabilities, activation slopes and vanishing gradients, gradient checking.

  5. Calculus 2Many inputs: partials, gradients, Jacobians

    Slopes on a landscape you drag over, the gradients of MSE and weight matrices, the Jacobian, and backprop as vector-Jacobian products.

  6. Probability 1Probability and Bayes

    The rules, Monty Hall and the birthday problem simulated, Bayes against the base rate, a spam filter you train, and drawing samples from any distribution.

  7. Probability 2Describing data

    Mean, median, spread, box plots and density estimates on real subway data; covariance, correlation and the multivariate bell.

  8. Linear algebra 2Eigenvectors and PCA

    The arrows a matrix only stretches, a Markov chain ranking this site's pages, and squeezing data onto its most important axes.

  9. Linear algebra 3SVD: rotate, stretch, rotate

    Every matrix turns a circle into an ellipse, a photo rebuilt from its biggest layers, PCA done right, a tiny recommender that finds hidden tastes, the pseudo-inverse, and why LoRA fine-tunes with low rank.

Milestone: fit linear regression three ways, by solving equations, by gradient descent, and by checking the gradient numerically.

Stage 2 · How a network learns

Neurons, backprop, losses and why training works or blows up.

  1. Chapter 3A neuron from scratch

    Weights, a bias and a bend: one neuron learning to separate two clouds of points, live.

  2. Chapter 4Backprop you can watch

    The chain rule as a relay of blame through a small network, step by step.

  3. Calculus 3Taylor series and curvature

    Why the learning rate must stay under 2/k, zigzags in stretched valleys, Newton's method, and saddles.

  4. Chapter 5Probability and loss

    Softmax, cross-entropy, and why a model "guesses" instead of knowing.

  5. Calculus 4Integrals: adding up the infinitely small

    Strips under a curve, densities and the bell curve, and why a minibatch's noise shrinks like 1/√N.

  6. Probability 3From data to parameters: MLE and MAP

    The central limit theorem, where MSE and cross-entropy come from, and regularization as a prior.

  7. Probability 5Information: entropy, cross-entropy and KL

    How many bits a subway station name needs, the extra bits a wrong belief costs (and why one unseen bracket makes the loss infinite), covering versus choosing, how a tree picks its question, and where training loss, perplexity, distillation and the fine-tuning leash come from.

  8. Calculus 5Convexity and constraints

    When a loss is one bowl (linear and logistic regression) and why a network's never is, how L2 makes the answer unique, the best point on a boundary (Lagrange, and PCA once more), and why lasso sets weights exactly to zero.

  9. Deep networksDeep networks, layer by layer

    An L-layer network trained in the page: a whole batch as one matrix, getting the shapes right, why random init and why depth.

  10. PyTorchTraining in PyTorch

    Tensors on a GPU, autograd, nn.Module, Dataset and DataLoader, the training loop with epochs and batches, saving and loading: the deep network you just built, written the way it's done at work.

Milestone: a multi-layer network written from scratch that learns a spiral, with its gradients checked.

At the end, you can

  • Frame a problem as ML (or decide it shouldn't be), pick a metric that matches the cost of mistakes, and set a baseline.
  • Load, clean and split real data without leaking the answer, in pandas.
  • Train and tune linear models, boosted trees and neural networks, in scikit-learn and PyTorch, and explain what every line does down to the gradient.
  • Diagnose a model: bias or variance, error analysis, learning curves, a loss curve that's gone wrong.
  • Use pretrained vision and language models: transfer learning, embeddings, retrieval, fine-tuning, and evals for LLM features.
  • Ship a model behind an API and monitor it, and say with numbers whether a new version is better.
