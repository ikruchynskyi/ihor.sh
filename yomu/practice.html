How this works (and why)

Recall, don't recognize. Typing the answer from memory is harder than picking it from four, and that effort is what makes it stick. Choosing is there for days when typing is too much.

Both directions. Reading Japanese (日本語 → English) and saying it (English → 日本語) are different skills; mixed rounds train both.

Spacing. Each word comes back just before you'd forget it: a day, then a few, then weeks. A new word reappears a few cards later in the same round, and a miss comes back until you get it. Grade honestly: "Hard" and "Again" are how the schedule learns.

Kanji are clickable. Every kanji opens its page: readings, stroke order, other words using it, real sentences. Knowing a kanji's meaning makes the next ten words that use it easier.

Typing Japanese: kana, kanji or romaji all work (shinbun or sinbun, toukyou or tokyo). Keys: Enter check / next, 1–4 grade. Word lists: JLPT lists by Jonathan Waller with JMdict glosses (EDRDG, CC BY-SA).
