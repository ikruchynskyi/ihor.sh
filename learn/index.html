Learn & build

The learning paths behind everything else on this site, published as I go. Every lesson is used somewhere real: filters in the radio, statistics on the NYC archives, a driver for my own USB dongle. Same approach as the other worlds: picture first, then the math, and everything live.

⚡ Electronics from scratch

Circuits you can tune, solved live by a small circuit simulator written for this course. Or build your own in the circuit lab: 26 parts from batteries and signal generators to MOSFETs, op-amps, 555 timers, regulators and logic gates, a 4-channel oscilloscope, and ready-made circuits (rectifiers, filters, amplifiers, a blinker, a 5 V supply) to drop in and change.

  1. Voltage, current, resistance

    Ohm's law with current you can watch, power and heat, voltage dividers and reading a light sensor, parallel branches, and the resistor every LED needs.

  2. Capacitors and time

    Charging curves, the RC time constant, and the simplest filter: the same one the radio course uses.

    Used in: the radio's filters, debouncing buttons, power supply smoothing.

  3. Coils, resonance and antennas

    Inductors, LC tanks ringing at one frequency, and why an antenna's length matters.

    Used in: tuning the RTL-SDR, Meshtastic antennas.

  4. Diodes and transistors

    One-way valves, and a MOSFET as a switch a microcontroller can flip, with the flyback diode it needs.

  5. Real parts

    Color codes and why values come in 4.7k (E-series), tolerance, power ratings and heat, capacitor types and DC bias, batteries that sag, wire gauge, and reading a datasheet.

  6. The circuit-theory toolkit

    Kirchhoff's laws, Thevenin and Norton, superposition, and AC as spinning arrows (phasors and impedance).

  7. Op-amps

    The golden rules, inverting and non-inverting amplifiers, comparators, integrators.

  8. Filters

    Passive and active filters, Bode plots, Q, and choosing one for audio or radio.

  9. Oscillators and timers

    RC and crystal oscillators, the 555 in astable and one-shot modes.

  10. Power supplies

    Linear regulators and dropout, buck and boost converters, efficiency and heat.

  11. Light and sensors

    LEDs, photodiodes and optocouplers; thermistors, Hall sensors, strain gauges, and reading them with an ADC.

  12. From gates to a computer

    Logic gates, flip-flops, counters, shift registers, an adder and a tiny CPU.

  13. Microcontrollers and buses

    GPIO pins as register bits, pull-ups and floating inputs, PWM, and UART, I²C and SPI on a logic analyzer you decode yourself.

  14. Motors

    Back-EMF and stall current, an H-bridge you switch by hand (and the shoot-through to avoid), PWM speed, servo pulses and stepper sequences.

  15. Hands-on

    Which breadboard holes connect, the multimeter without blown fuses, a soldering game, scope triggering and probes, and KiCad from schematic to sized traces.

  16. Audio

    Microphones (and yours, live), decibels, amplifier classes A, B, AB and D drawn and played out loud with their harmonics, and speakers: power into 4 and 8 Ω.
