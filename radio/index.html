A software-defined radio receiver, one stage at a time. Load a recording from an RTL-SDR (or use the built-in demo signals), click a signal in the waterfall to tune it, and see what each step does to it.

0Input: complex I/Q samples How & why →

An SDR mixes the antenna signal with its center frequency and samples two channels, I and Q, 90° apart. Because each sample is a complex number, the spectrum covers both sides of the center: −fs/2 to +fs/2.

Live mode: plug in an RTL-SDR (R820T/R820T2 tuner) and press Connect. Our own driver runs in the browser over WebUSB, so there's nothing to install and your samples never leave your computer. Chrome, Edge or Opera. Windows: install the WinUSB driver once with Zadig. Linux: unload the dvb_usb_rtl28xxu kernel module.

1Spectrum & waterfall How & why →

Each row is one FFT, with time running downward. Brighter means stronger. Click a signal to tune it.

2Mixer: move the signal to 0 Hz How & why →

Multiplying by e−j2πf·t slides the whole spectrum left by f, so the station you picked now sits at DC.

3Low-pass filter How & why →

A windowed-sinc FIR (orange) keeps ±bandwidth/2 around DC and attenuates everything else by ~50 dB. More taps give a sharper edge but cost more compute.

4Decimate How & why →

Now that nothing outside the passband is left to alias, keep only every M-th sample. The view zooms in: the same station at a much lower sample rate.
