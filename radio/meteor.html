Meteor & weather satellite recorder

Catch Russia's Meteor-M2 3 and 4 weather satellites with an RTL-SDR: digital LRPT pictures at 137.9 MHz (offset QPSK, 72,000 symbols/s, about 110 kHz wide). Watch the orbit on the map, decode the pictures right here, and record the raw signal to process in SatDump. (NOAA 15, 18 and 19, the analog APT satellites, were switched off in 2025.) Over you : for your own USB dongle
NYC Reception Note: In NYC, FM broadcast filters (like Nooelec Flamingo) notch out 88–108 MHz, but high RF environments also have heavy pager transmitters (152–158 MHz), NOAA weather radio (162.4–162.55 MHz) and aviation (118–137 MHz). If RTL-SDR gain is set too high (e.g. max 49 dB), these out-of-band signals saturate the 8-bit ADC and bury the satellite in intermodulation noise (destroying the 4-dot constellation). Keep dongle gain moderate (about 20–36 dB) and watch the input level line under the recording button: it warns when samples clip. Meteor LRPT decodes best on passes above 35° with a clear view of the sky.

    Turning it into a picture

    • This page decodes Meteor LRPT directly in your browser. For advanced offline processing, open recorded .cu8 files in SatDump:
      • Meteor-M2 3 / 4: Select Offline processing → METEOR M2-x LRPT 72k, baseband format cu8, and the sample rate in the file name (204800 with a USB dongle at its usual 1.024 MS/s).
    • Antenna: a horizontal V-dipole (two 53 cm rods at 120° angle, oriented north-south) or QFH beats a whip antenna on a windowsill. Passes below 20° are subject to terrestrial obstruction and urban RF interference.
    • Files are about 25 MB a minute and live directly in browser memory (nothing is uploaded to a remote server): recordings stop automatically at 20 minutes, and the tab keeps the last 3. Click Download to save to disk.
