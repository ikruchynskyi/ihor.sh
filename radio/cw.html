CW trainer

Morse code, called CW on the air, is still how radio amateurs reach the other side of the world with a few watts. Learn it by ear the way the experts recommend (the Koch method: full speed from day one, two letters first, one more each time you copy 90%), then practice copying and sending at any speed. Send with your keyboard, mouse, a touchscreen, or a real key on USB.

Lesson

The drill plays 5 groups of 5 letters. Type as you hear, don't stop on a missed letter. 90% unlocks the next one.

Using a real key

Most USB Morse key adapters show up as a mouse (left button = dit or straight key, right button = dah) or as a keyboard (Ctrl keys, [ ], or Z X). This page listens to all of them while the Send tab is open, so plug it in and key away. A straight key also works on Space; paddles on Z/X, [/] or left/right Ctrl. If dits and dahs come out swapped, tick Swap paddles.

Why it's taught this way

If you learn Morse from a chart of dots and dashes, you end up counting them in your head, and you hit a wall around 10 words per minute. The Koch method avoids that: every letter is played at the speed you'll eventually use (20 WPM is a good target), so you learn its sound as a whole, like a word. Farnsworth spacing stretches only the gaps between letters, giving you thinking time without slowing the letters down; close the gap as you improve.

How the timing works and how a receiver turns a beep back into text is in Radio from scratch, chapter 10.
