Meteor station check

This page checks the station that records Meteor weather satellites: a V-dipole on a balcony in New York, a SAWbird amplifier and filter, an FM band-stop filter, and a HackRF on a server. It measures what can be measured from here, reads every recorded pass back to see where in the sky the antenna hears the satellites, and says what to change. A check runs by itself every half hour when the radio is free; you can run one now (it borrows the radio for about two seconds, never during a pass).

The radio and its antenna chain

One second of everything the radio hears, 9.6 MHz around 137.5 MHz. Outside the amplifier's filter you see the receiver's own noise (green line); inside, the antenna's noise lifted by the amplifier (yellow). The two Meteor channels, 137.1 and 137.9 MHz, are shaded; red marks are narrow peaks (possible interference). The numbers are explained in course chapter 3 and chapter 12.

Where the antenna hears the satellites

Every recorded pass, read back: the strongest signal-meter reading in each direction and height of the sky (north up, the horizon at the edge, straight up in the middle). Decoding needs about 3.5. Gray: the satellite passed there but was barely heard. The dashed outline is the recorder's window: passes that spend enough time inside it get recorded.
