Ham radio license, visually

In the US, anyone can earn an amateur radio license by passing a multiple-choice exam: Technician first, then General, then Amateur Extra, each opening more of the radio spectrum. There's no Morse test any more, and every possible question is public: the official question pools are released into the public domain, word for word.

That makes it tempting to memorize answers. This page helps you do better: practice the real questions with spaced repetition, take mock exams built exactly like the real ones, and when an answer doesn't make sense, open the visual lesson that explains it.

How the prediction works

The real exam takes one random question from each group of the pool. So for every group, your chance of getting its question right is the average over its questions: about 25% for questions you've never seen (a guess), rising as you answer them correctly on separate days (40% just after a miss, then 65%, 80%, 88%, 94%, 97%). The exam score is then a sum of one weighted coin per group, and the chart shows the exact odds of every possible total. It's deliberately a little pessimistic.

The same idea picks your new questions: since every group gives exactly one exam question, learning a question from a small group raises your expected score more than one from a big group. Small groups come first, a few groups at a time.

    Taking the real exam

    • Exams are given by volunteer examiners (VEs), in person or online. Find a session through a VE coordinator such as the ARRL or HamStudy's session list.
    • Before the exam, get an FCC Registration Number (FRN) in the FCC's CORES system. After you pass, the FCC charges a $35 application fee and your call sign appears in its database, usually within days. A license lasts 10 years.
    • You can take several elements at one session: pass Technician, and you may try General right away.
