SSTV decoder

Slow-scan TV sends a still picture as sound: a tone whose pitch is the brightness, line by line. Astronauts on the International Space Station send SSTV pictures on 145.800 MHz FM during special events, and a $30 dongle can receive them. This page decodes them in your browser. How it works, step by step, is in Radio from scratch, chapter 17.

Catching the ISS

  • ARISS announces SSTV events a few times a year, usually sending a series of pictures in PD120 for several days. Their gallery and announcements: ariss.org.
  • The ISS is above your horizon for up to about 10 minutes per pass; a PD120 picture takes about 2 minutes, so you'll catch several per good pass. Pass times: any satellite tracker (the ISS is the easiest object in the sky to predict).
  • Use narrow FM, no squelch (it would chop the picture), and a vertical antenna or better. Doppler shift is a few kHz on 2 meters (↩ why), which narrow FM tolerates.
  • Receiving the ISS needs no license; transmitting to it does (Technician or higher).
