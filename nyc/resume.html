How a hiring system reads your résumé

Most applications are read by software (an applicant tracking system) before a person sees them. It pulls out plain text, guesses your name, contact details, jobs and skills, and often filters on the exact words a posting uses. This page shows you that plain text, finds the layout choices that break it, checks the basics, and, given a job, lists the terms you're missing. It never invents anything for you to claim.

    Against a job

    Paste a job posting (or open one from the jobs page) to see which of its words and skills your résumé has. Many systems match exact words, so "Kubernetes" in the posting needs "Kubernetes" in your résumé, not just "k8s". Add only what's true.

    🎯 The best-matching version for this job

    The AI rebuilds your résumé around the job above: your own roles and dates, the bullets that matter for it first and worded the way it words things, a summary, and the job's skills added to your skills list. Then untick every skill you don't have: it leaves the list, and any line that named it goes back to your own words. Tailored résumés get about 60% more interview callbacks than generic ones, but 63% of job seekers who used AI on theirs say it added false details (Huntr, 2026), and an interviewer will ask about every line.
